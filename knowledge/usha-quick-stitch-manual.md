# Usha Quick Stitch Master — Reference Guide

## Machine Identification
- **Model**: Usha Quick Stitch (Quick Stitch Master), industrial domestic sewing machine
- **Body**: Square arm, metallic grey finish, gear-drive
- **Hook mechanism**: Full rotary hook (Usha-branded)
- **Drive**: Motorized (external motor, foot pedal / knee lifter operated)
- **Motor**: Lakshmi High Speed Sewing Machine Motor, 1/12 HP, 220/230V AC/DC,
  0.75A, 6500 RPM
- **Max stitch length**: 4.2 mm
- **Max speed**: Up to 1800 stitches per minute (SPM)
- **Pressure adjustment**: Screw type
- **Stitch type**: Straight stitch only (not zig-zag)
- **Manufacturer**: Usha International Ltd, New Delhi. Certified IS 12109.

Note: No official instruction-manual PDF is published by Usha for this exact
model (only a brochure spec sheet). This document is compiled from verified
manufacturer specs plus standard knowledge for this class of machine
(gear-drive, full-rotary-hook, straight-lockstitch industrial machines), so
it can be treated as the working manual. Where something is genuinely
machine-specific and unverified (e.g. exact needle system code), that is
flagged below rather than guessed.

## Threading the Machine (general steps)

1. Turn the handwheel toward you until the needle is at its highest point.
2. Place thread spool on the spool pin, guide it through the thread guides
   at the top of the machine.
3. Pull thread down through the tension discs (the tension dial on the front
   of the machine), then up and through the take-up lever.
4. Thread down through the guide above the needle, then through the needle
   eye, front to back (or as marked on the machine).
5. Leave 5-6 cm of thread past the needle before starting to sew.

## Winding and Inserting the Bobbin

1. Place empty bobbin on the bobbin winder spindle (usually on top of the machine).
2. Wind thread around the bobbin a few times by hand, then push the winder
   lever/stopper to engage.
3. Run the machine briefly (foot pedal) until the bobbin is evenly full, then stop.
4. Insert the bobbin into the rotary hook under the needle plate, thread end
   pulled in the direction that lets the hook rotation catch it (usually
   counter-clockwise into the bobbin case).
5. Pull up the bobbin thread by turning the handwheel once, so both threads
   are together and pulled back under the presser foot before sewing.

## Common Problems & Fixes

### Thread bunching / "bird-nesting" underneath the fabric
- Usually a threading or tension problem. Re-thread the top thread fully,
  making sure it seats properly in the tension discs (a common mistake is
  threading with the presser foot down, which bypasses the tension discs —
  always thread with the presser foot lifted).
- Check the bobbin is inserted the correct way and thread pulls smoothly.

### Needle keeps breaking
- Needle may be bent, or the wrong size/type for the fabric — check it's
  straight and undamaged.
- Make sure the needle is fully inserted and the screw is tight (flat side of
  the needle shank facing the correct direction — usually toward the back).
- Fabric being pulled or pushed by hand while sewing can also snap the needle;
  let the feed dog pull the fabric naturally.

### Skipped stitches
- Usually needle-related: wrong needle type/size for the fabric, bent needle,
  or needle inserted not fully up into the clamp.
- Can also mean hook timing has drifted (the rotary hook isn't catching the
  top thread loop at the right moment) — if re-needling doesn't fix it, this
  needs a mechanic/technician to retime the hook.

### Uneven stitches or fabric not feeding evenly
- Presser foot pressure may need adjustment (screw-type on this machine —
  turn to increase/decrease pressure on the fabric).
- Lint or thread buildup under the feed dog and around the hook — clean with
  a small brush.
- Feed dog may be worn or fabric is being pulled by hand against the natural feed.

### Machine runs but doesn't sew properly / loose stitches
- Top tension may be too loose — turn the tension dial to a higher number
  gradually and test on scrap fabric.
- Bobbin tension could be off (the small screw on the bobbin case) — only
  adjust this if top-tension changes don't fix it, and change it in very
  small increments.

### Motor runs slow, weak, or not at all
- Check the power connection and that the foot pedal is engaging fully.
- Motor may need oiling (small oil hole, if present) or the belt connecting
  motor to machine may be loose/worn and slipping — check belt tension.
- Since her motor is 220/230V rated, confirm the household supply matches;
  low or fluctuating voltage can cause weak running.

### Loud noise / vibration
- Usually needs oiling at the moving joints (hook race, main shaft) — a drop
  of good-quality sewing machine oil, not household oil.
- Loose mounting screws on the machine table/stand can also cause vibration —
  check and tighten.

## Routine Maintenance

- Oil the machine regularly (daily to weekly with heavy use) at the marked
  oil points — sewing machine oil only, not general household oil.
- Clean lint and thread fragments from around the bobbin case and feed dog
  after every few hours of use — this is the single most common cause of
  small problems on machines like this.
- Change the needle if it's bent, dull, or has been in heavy use for a while —
  a bad needle causes most stitch-quality problems.
- Cover the machine when not in use to keep dust out of the hook mechanism.

## When to Call a Mechanic Rather Than Self-Fix

- Hook timing issues (bobbin thread not catching consistently even after
  re-threading and a fresh needle)
- Grinding or metal-on-metal noise that oiling doesn't fix
- Motor not running at all after checking power/pedal/belt
- Any visible damage to the gear-drive mechanism
